# Honest Review: The Blended Reason

Checking [output.md](output.md) against what [case.md](case.md) was built to test.

## What Worked

- **Did not force a single classification onto a genuinely mixed reason.** All four cases in the first example had one dominant, identifiable factor. This one deliberately does not, and the output correctly resisted picking the easiest-sounding one (the schedule conflict, since it's concrete) and declaring the whole thing solved by addressing that.
- **Separated the concrete factor from the vaguer ones.** The schedule conflict got treated differently from "a different direction" and "group dynamic," which is exactly the fact-from-assumption discipline this whole tool is meant to apply, not everything stated together carries equal evidential weight.
- **Was honest about what trying again would and would not address.** It did not claim that fixing the schedule conflict would resolve the whole rejection, since the other two factors were never confirmed or denied.

## What Still Needs a Human Check

- Whether it is worth asking directly which factor actually mattered most, rather than guessing from a blended stated reason, is a judgement call only the person themselves can make.

## Verdict

No automatic failure. This is the harder case the first four cases deliberately avoided: a reason that blends multiple factors with no clear primary one, correctly left unresolved rather than tidied into a false, single explanation.
