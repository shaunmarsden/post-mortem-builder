# Review: The Blended Reason

I checked [output.md](output.md) against what [case.md](case.md) was built to test.

## What Worked

It didn't force one classification onto a mixed reason. All four cases in the first example had one clear main factor. This one deliberately doesn't. The output didn't pick the easiest-sounding factor (the schedule conflict, because it's concrete) and declare the whole thing solved by dealing with that.

It kept the concrete factor apart from the vaguer ones. It treated the schedule conflict differently from "a different direction" and "group dynamic," which is the whole point of the tool: separate fact from assumption, because not everything stated together carries the same weight as evidence.

It was straight about what trying again would and wouldn't address. It didn't claim that fixing the schedule conflict would settle the whole rejection, since nobody confirmed or ruled out the other two factors.

## What Still Needs a Human Check

- Only the person can judge whether it's worth asking directly which factor mattered most, rather than guessing from a blended reason. SKILL.md asks the output to name what would resolve the uncertainty, and this one doesn't. Asking which factor decided it is the obvious step.

## Verdict

No automatic failure. This is the harder case the first four deliberately avoided: a reason that blends several factors with no clear main one. The output rightly left it unresolved rather than tidying it into a single false explanation.
