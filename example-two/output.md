# Post-Mortem: The Blended Reason

Source: [case.md](case.md).

## Do Not Collapse This Into One Classification

Three separate factors are stated together: "a different direction" (vague, could mean almost anything), the schedule conflicts (a real, specific, stated constraint), and the group dynamic with the existing cast (a fit-with-the-team reason). Picking one of these to classify the whole outcome, calling this purely `hard-blocker` because of the schedule conflict, or purely `contact-change`-adjacent because of "group dynamic," would flatten a genuinely blended reason into a single tidy story the evidence does not actually support that cleanly.

## What Can Be Said Honestly

- The schedule conflict is the one concrete, verifiable factor named. If that were the only issue, it would point toward `hard-blocker` or `timing`, depending on whether the schedule conflict is genuinely fixed or could change for a future production.
- "A different direction" and "group dynamic" are vaguer and could each independently explain the outcome; neither is confirmed as the deciding factor over the others.
- Because three factors were named together with no indication of which one actually decided it, this is best recorded as **unresolved, multiple factors stated, none confirmed as primary**, rather than forced into one of the five clean categories.

## What Would Justify Trying Again

If a future production has a schedule that genuinely avoids the stated conflict, that would address the one concrete factor named, though the other two vaguer reasons would remain unaddressed and unconfirmed either way. Trying again should not lean on having "solved" this rejection, since it was never cleanly diagnosed to begin with.
