# Post-Mortem Builder

You do not need to install anything to try this once: copy this whole file, paste it as your first message in any AI chat tool, then follow it with your actual inputs.

A stated reason for a failure is not always the whole story, and the person who said no is not always the whole story either. This tells the difference between something genuinely over and something only blocked, before either chasing something that has clearly ended or writing off something that has not.

## Gather the Inputs

- The final message, stated reason, or record marking the outcome
- Anything known about what changed on the other side: a person leaving, a reorganisation, a budget cycle, a policy change
- Whatever evidence was originally built for this attempt, so it is clear what, if anything, is still reusable

## Separate Fact from Assumption

Start with exactly what was said or shown, not the story that would make the most sense of it. A stated reason often blends more than one factor together. Do not collapse it into a single tidy explanation the evidence does not actually support.

## Check Whether the Underlying Problem Still Exists

A person leaving, a reorganisation, or one specific concern landing badly does not automatically mean the actual need or opportunity this attempt was addressing has gone away. Check whether that underlying case still stands on its own, independent of who raised it or who was involved.

## Classify the Outcome

Work out which of these is the closest fit, and say so honestly rather than defaulting to whichever feels more comfortable:

- `hard-blocker`: the fit was never really there, or a real requirement was not met and is unlikely to change
- `timing`: the fit is real, but now is not the right moment; a stated future cycle or a stated constraint usually means exactly that, not a polite no
- `contact-change`: the specific person this ran through is no longer the route in, but the underlying case may still be usable through someone else
- `unresolved-concern`: something specific was raised, an answer was actually given back, and it was still rejected
- `no-decision`: something specific was sent back and then contact from both sides simply stops. Nobody said no. This is a live thread nobody returned to, not a quiet decision against it. The next action is following up on the answer already sent, not waiting indefinitely

## Decide What Would Justify Trying Again

Do not default to either extreme: writing it off forever, or trying again immediately. If a real, specific trigger exists, a named future point, a new contact, name it. If there genuinely is not one, say that plainly instead of inventing a hopeful reason to soften the outcome.

## Apply the Guardrails

- Never invent the reason for the outcome beyond what was actually said or shown
- Never invent a specific future date or trigger where only a vague one was given; treat vague as vague
- Never assume an earlier, separate concern was the deciding factor without evidence that it was
- Never let this become a reason to re-approach someone who has given a clear, unconditional no

## Stop When the Task Is Unsafe

Do not produce a confident classification when:

- The stated reason is too thin or too vague to distinguish a hard blocker from timing
- Conflicting evidence exists about why it failed, and nothing resolves the conflict
- The request is to manufacture a reason to try again with someone who has clearly and unconditionally said no

Say so, and name exactly what would resolve the uncertainty, rather than picking a classification to seem decisive.

## Require Human Review

This produces an analysis and a recommendation. Any actual decision to try again, and any message sent, stays a deliberate human choice.
